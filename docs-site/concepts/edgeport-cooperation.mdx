When you run more than one EdgePort, a caller and a callee can be connected to different EdgePorts. Routr delivers the call anyway: the EdgePort that receives the request forwards it to the EdgePort that holds the callee's connection. You don't need a SIP-aware load balancer in front of Routr for this.

## Why the request must reach the right EdgePort

A phone behind NAT can usually be reached only through the connection, or NAT mapping, it opened itself. If Bob registered through `edgeport-02` over TCP, only `edgeport-02` holds that connection. A request for Bob that lands on `edgeport-01` can't simply be sent to Bob's address: there's no connection there, and his NAT router would drop it.

## How EdgePorts cooperate

1. **Registration records the EdgePort.** When Bob registers, the Location Service stores his route together with the `edgePortRef` of the EdgePort that received the `REGISTER`, plus that EdgePort's listening points, external addresses, and local networks. An EdgePort's ref comes from the `ref` in its configuration, or from the `HOSTNAME` environment variable when it's set. On Kubernetes, that's the pod name.
2. **Connect compares refs.** Alice's `INVITE` lands on `edgeport-01`. The Connect processor looks up Bob, gets his route, and sees that the route's `edgePortRef` is `edgeport-02`, not the EdgePort the request came from.
3. **Connect sends the request to the peer EdgePort.** Instead of routing straight to Bob, Connect adds its own `Via` and `Record-Route`, then a `Route` header pointing at `edgeport-02`, followed by a `Route` to Bob. It also adds an `X-Edgeport-Ref` header and decreases `Max-Forwards`.
4. **The peer EdgePort delivers it.** `edgeport-01` sends the request to `edgeport-02`. There, Connect sees `X-Edgeport-Ref`, confirms the request comes from a trusted peer EdgePort, rebuilds the route from the message without running routing again, and `edgeport-02` delivers the `INVITE` to Bob over his existing connection.

Because Connect adds `Record-Route` headers, later requests in the same dialog, such as `ACK` and `BYE`, follow the same EdgePorts.

## Trusting peer EdgePorts

A request with `X-Edgeport-Ref` skips routing and access checks on the second EdgePort. Anyone can put that header in a SIP message, so honoring it from any source would let an attacker relay calls through Routr for free (toll fraud).

The Connect processor honors `X-Edgeport-Ref` only from addresses you list. Set these keys in the standalone Connect's [`connect.yaml`](/reference/configuration-files#connect-yaml), or the `connect.trustedEdgePortAddresses` and `connect.trustedEdgePorts` Helm values:

| Key | Description |
| --- | --- |
| `spec.trustedEdgePortAddresses` | IPs, CIDRs or DNS names of your EdgePorts. Connect compares them with the request's source address: the top `Via` header's `received` value, or its host when `received` is absent. A name is resolved at start and every 10 seconds; one that stops resolving keeps its last addresses for 30 seconds, then trusts nothing. Empty by default in `connect.yaml`, which trusts no peer EdgePort. The Helm chart's default is its EdgePorts' headless Service, which resolves to exactly its EdgePort pods. |
| `spec.trustedEdgePorts` | Optional. EdgePort refs. When set, the `X-Edgeport-Ref` value must also be one of them. |

When a request carries `X-Edgeport-Ref` but doesn't come from a trusted address, Connect removes the header, logs a warning, and routes the request normally with the usual access checks. `X-Edgeport-Ref` is also on the default list of platform headers Connect removes from untrusted sources (see [SIP headers](/reference/sip-headers)).

<Warning>
  The Helm chart trusts its own EdgePorts by name. A standalone Connect with several EdgePorts must list their addresses or a name that resolves to them in `trustedEdgePortAddresses`, as the [Connect specification](https://github.com/fonoster/routr/blob/main/CONNECT.md) requires. Never list a whole pod network: clients that reach a NodePort can arrive from addresses inside it (SNAT, a CNI tunnel) and would be trusted. RoutrOne runs a single EdgePort and has no such key.
</Warning>

## Use connection-oriented transports

EdgePort cooperation relies on the callee's connection or NAT mapping staying open. Use TCP, TLS, WS, or WSS for your clients whenever you can.

UDP has no connection. A NAT router keeps a mapping (a pinhole) open for UDP only while traffic flows, and many routers close it after about 30 seconds of silence. After that, Routr can't reach the phone until it sends something again. If you must use UDP, set the clients' `REGISTER` expiry, or re-register interval, below 30 seconds so the pinhole stays open.

## When an EdgePort restarts

The EdgePort is the only stateful service in Routr. It keeps SIP transactions and client connections in memory, and they don't survive a restart:

- **Calls in progress on that EdgePort are lost.** Requests and responses for those calls have no transaction or connection to go back to. Routr doesn't move calls to another EdgePort.
- **Its registrations become unreachable.** The Location Service may still hold routes that point at the restarted EdgePort, but the connections behind them are gone. Each client must reconnect and re-register, through the restarted EdgePort or another one, before it can receive calls again. How fast that happens depends on the client's re-register interval and how quickly it notices the lost connection.

Plan maintenance accordingly: drain or schedule EdgePort restarts when few calls are active, and keep client re-register intervals short.
