Connect mode is Routr running with its built-in **Connect processor**. Connect implements the PBX portion of the SIP Connect 1.1 specification, so you can register phones, call between them, reach the PSTN through a carrier, and send calls to media servers by creating resources instead of writing code. It's the most common way to run Routr, and both the `routr-one` image and the `routr-connect` Helm chart use it.

## Resources

Connect describes your network with seven kinds of resources. You manage them with [rctl](/reference/rctl), the [SDK](/reference/sdk), or, with the `simpledata` API Server, YAML files.

| Resource | What it represents | Relates to |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Domain](/reference/resources/domains) | A group of Agents that can call each other, identified by a SIP URI such as `sip.local`. | An optional ACL, and egress policies that map dialed numbers (by regular expression) to a Number for outbound calls. |
| [Agent](/reference/resources/agents) | A SIP endpoint that a person uses, such as a softphone, desk phone, or paging speaker. | Belongs to one Domain and authenticates with Credentials. |
| [Credentials](/reference/resources/credentials) | A username and password. | Used by Agents and Peers to register, and by Trunks for inbound and outbound authentication. |
| [ACL](/reference/resources/acl) | An access control list of allowed and denied networks. | Referenced by Domains, Peers, and Trunks. |
| [Peer](/reference/resources/peers) | A trusted SIP endpoint outside any Domain, usually a media server, PBX, or feature server such as Asterisk or FreeSWITCH. | Registers under a shared address of record (`aor`) with Credentials, and can load balance across every instance that does. |
| [Trunk](/reference/resources/trunks) | A connection to a SIP carrier, gateway, or SBC that reaches the PSTN. | Uses Credentials and an ACL for inbound calls, and a list of outbound URIs. With `sendRegister`, the [Registry](/reference/components/registry) registers with the carrier for you. |
| [Number](/reference/resources/numbers) | A phone number (`telUrl`) you own through a Trunk. | Belongs to a Trunk. Its `aorLink` sends inbound calls to an Agent or Peer, and Domains use it as the caller identity for outbound calls. |

Read [Resources overview](/reference/resources/overview) for every field.

## How Connect processes a request

Connect handles each request according to its method:

- **`REGISTER`**: Connect finds the Agent or Peer by the user and host in the `From` header, checks the digest credentials, and stores the route in the [Location Service](/reference/components/location). Agents register under their own address; Peers register under their `aor`.
- **Requests inside a dialog**: any request whose `To` header carries a tag, such as a re-INVITE, `BYE`, `REFER`, or `NOTIFY`, is forwarded along the path already recorded for the dialog. Connect forwards `ACK` and `BYE` this way even without a tag.
- **`CANCEL`**: Connect doesn't route it as a new call. It follows the `INVITE` it cancels, on the same path.
- **`INVITE` and other new requests**: Connect identifies the caller and the callee, picks one of five [routing types](/concepts/routing), runs the access checks for that type, finds the route, and returns the request with the alterations needed to reach the target.
- **`MESSAGE`, `SUBSCRIBE`, `NOTIFY`, `PUBLISH`, and `REFER` outside a dialog**: Connect answers `405 Method Not Allowed`. To handle them, put another Processor in front of Connect in the Dispatcher.

Agents that can't hold a password, such as browser clients, can authenticate with a signed JWT in the `X-Connect-Token` header instead. The token carries claims similar to an Agent's fields: its `aor`, `domain`, `allowedMethods`, and an `aorLink` to the Peer it may call. See [Ephemeral agents in the browser](/tutorials/ephemeral-agents-in-the-browser).

## When to use Connect and when to write a Processor

Use Connect when your network fits the PBX model:

- Phones that register and call each other inside a Domain.
- Calls to and from the PSTN through carrier Trunks and Numbers.
- A group of Asterisk or FreeSWITCH servers behind Routr, with load balancing and session affinity.
- WebRTC clients, together with RTPRelay and RTPEngine.

Write your own Processor when your routing doesn't fit those resources, for example routing by a database lookup, a time of day, or an external API, or handling methods Connect rejects such as `MESSAGE`. You don't have to choose one or the other: the Dispatcher can send some requests to your Processor with a `matchFunc` and the rest to Connect. See [Build a processor](/guides/build-a-processor).
